# IBM HR Analytics – Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview
This project analyzes employee attrition using Python to identify patterns and factors associated with employees leaving an organization.

The analysis focuses on employee demographics, job roles, departments, monthly income, overtime, and work experience.

## Objectives
- Analyze overall employee attrition.
- Identify departments and job roles with higher attrition rates.
- Understand the relationship between age and employee attrition.
- Analyze the impact of monthly income and overtime.
- Explore attrition patterns based on work experience.
- Provide business recommendations to improve employee retention.

## Dataset
**Dataset:** IBM HR Analytics – Employee Attrition

The dataset contains employee information, including:
- Age
- Department
- Job Role
- Monthly Income
- OverTime
- Total Working Years
- Attrition

## Key Business Insights

| Metric | Finding |
|---|---:|
| Overall Attrition Rate | 16.12% |
| Sales Department Attrition | 20.63% |
| Sales Representative Attrition | 39.76% |
| Age Group 18–25 Attrition | 34.78% |
| Income Group ₹0–₹3,000 Attrition | 28.61% |
| Overtime Employees Attrition | 30.53% |
| Employees with 0–5 Years Experience | 28.20% |

## Business Recommendations
- Improve retention strategies for younger employees.
- Review workload and overtime policies.
- Evaluate compensation for lower-income employees.
- Investigate higher attrition in the Sales department.
- Provide career development and training opportunities.

## Conclusion
The analysis identifies several employee groups with comparatively higher attrition rates, particularly younger employees, lower-income employees, employees working overtime, and employees with less work experience.

Organizations can use these insights to improve employee retention strategies, review compensation policies, manage workloads, and provide better career development opportunities.
